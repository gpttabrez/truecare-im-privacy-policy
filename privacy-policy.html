Privacy Policy

Truecare Hospitals — WhatsApp Business App Coexistence Integration

Effective date: 10 October 2026  ·  Last updated: 10 October 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how Truecare Hospitals ("we", "us", "our"), a multispecialty hospital located in Kolar, Karnataka, India, collects, uses, stores, and protects information in connection with its WhatsApp Business App Coexistence integration (the "Service"). The Service is a software integration, built on Meta's WhatsApp Business Platform and Supabase, that connects the hospital's existing WhatsApp Business app number to a backend so that authorised staff can complete onboarding and the backend can receive WhatsApp event notifications.

This policy describes only the data processed by the Service's technical integration. Messages that end users exchange with the hospital over WhatsApp are governed by WhatsApp's and Meta's own terms and privacy policies, as well as the hospital's own patient-notice practices.

1. Scope and roles

The Service is operated by Truecare Hospitals, which owns the WhatsApp Business account and phone number connected through the Service. The Service stores operator account data and technical event data. It does not itself provide medical advice and does not store medical records.

Truecare Hospitals is the controller of personal data connected to its WhatsApp number, and this integration processes that data on its behalf. Data relating to individual patients is handled in accordance with the hospital's own patient-notice and consent practices.

2. Information we process

The Service processes the following categories of information. This list is derived from the Service's actual database schema and server components.

2.1 Operator account data

To authenticate the staff ("operators") who run onboarding, the Service uses email and password sign-in. We process:

  • Operator email address and an authentication credential managed by our authentication provider (passwords are handled by the provider and are not stored by the Service in readable form).
  • A generated operator user identifier (UUID) assigned by the authentication provider.

2.2 WhatsApp Business account and asset identifiers

When an operator completes Meta Embedded Signup, the Service reads and stores business/asset identifiers returned by Meta:

  • Business account identifier and WhatsApp Business Account ("WABA") identifier.
  • Phone number identifier, display phone number, and verified business name.
  • Platform type, business-app coexistence flag, account status, and an onboarding timestamp.

2.3 Onboarding session data

To bind an Embedded Signup exchange to the correct operator, the Service issues a single-use, random nonce and stores it with the operator user identifier, an expiry time (10 minutes), and a consumed time.

2.4 Access credentials (tokens)

The Service exchanges Meta's authorization code, server-side, for a WhatsApp Business access token. That token is stored encrypted in Supabase Vault. Only an opaque Vault reference (a UUID) plus non-sensitive lifecycle metadata (status such as none/active/expired/revoked, expiry time, and last-rotated time) is stored in the Service's ordinary database tables. The raw token is not stored in ordinary tables and is never exposed to the browser page.

2.5 WhatsApp webhook event data

Meta sends webhook notifications to the Service for the connected WhatsApp Business Account (for example, incoming message notifications and message status changes). For each event the Service may store:

  • Object type, event field/type, WABA identifier, phone number identifier.
  • Message identifier, delivery/status value, and event timestamp.
  • A de-duplication key derived by hashing the event's identity fields, plus a signature-validity flag.
  • Received/processed timestamps and a processing status.
  • A redacted copy of the event payload (see 2.6).

2.6 Redaction of payload content

Before any webhook payload is stored, the Service applies a redaction step implemented in code:

  • Message text and bodies, captions, media, files, links/URLs, contact cards, names, email addresses, and physical addresses are removed entirely ("dropped") and are not stored.
  • Phone-number-like identifiers (for example from, to, wa_id, recipient_id, display_phone_number, phone_number, customer_phone) are replaced with a truncated SHA-256 hash, not stored in plaintext.
  • Other string values are truncated to a maximum length, and nesting depth is capped, to limit what is retained.

2.7 Data we do not intentionally collect

The Service is not designed to collect special-category or medical information, and its redaction step is intended to strip message content, contact details, and raw phone numbers before storage.

3. How we use information

  • To authenticate operators and authorize actions by role (owner, admin, viewer).
  • To complete Embedded Signup and securely store the resulting access token.
  • To verify that the WABA and phone number being onboarded match the assets granted by the operator.
  • To receive, verify, de-duplicate, and record WhatsApp webhook events relevant to the connected number.
  • To maintain operational and security logs (for example, rejected webhook signatures).
  • To comply with legal obligations and to enforce our terms.

4. How we use Meta

The Service uses Meta's WhatsApp Business Platform and Graph API to: run the Meta Embedded Signup (Facebook Login for business) flow; exchange the resulting authorization code for a business access token; read the connected WhatsApp Business Account and phone number identifiers for verification; and subscribe the integration to receive webhook event notifications. Meta processes data under its own terms and privacy policy, available on Meta's websites. Meta is an independent controller/service provider for the data it processes on its platform.

5. How we use Supabase

The Service's backend is hosted on Supabase, which provides the managed PostgreSQL database, authentication, edge functions, and encrypted secret storage (Vault) used by the Service. Supabase processes this data on our behalf as a hosting/sub-processor.

7. Security measures

The Service implements the following technical measures. This description is factual and is not a guarantee of absolute security; no method of electronic storage or transmission is completely secure.

  • Webhook authenticity is verified using Meta's HMAC-SHA256 signature header (X-Hub-Signature-256) over the exact raw request body, with a timing-safe comparison.
  • Authorization codes and access tokens are exchanged and stored server-side; the raw token is held only in Supabase Vault (encrypted), referenced by an opaque UUID.
  • Database tables are protected with row-level security forced on, and are accessed through server-side functions rather than direct client access.
  • Operator actions are authorized by role; only operators with the owner role may perform token operations.
  • Application secrets (Meta app credentials, verification token, service credentials) are stored as server-side secrets and are not placed in client pages or the repository.

8. Retention

We retain information only as long as necessary to operate the Service, meet legal obligations, and resolve disputes. Onboarding session records expire after 10 minutes, and expired sessions are removed by the Service. Account and asset identifiers are retained while the number remains connected through the Service. Access tokens are held in encrypted storage until they are revoked (at which point they are deleted from our secret store) or until they expire. Webhook event records are retained for operational and security purposes.
