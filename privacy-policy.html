1. Who we are

TRUECARE MULTISPECIALITY HOSPITAL ("the Hospital", also trading as Truecare Hospitals, "we", "us", "our") is a multispeciality hospital located in Kolar, Karnataka, India. TRUECARE IM provides the technical WhatsApp Business integration (the "Service") for the Hospital, connecting the Hospital's existing WhatsApp Business app number to Meta's WhatsApp Business Platform and to a secure managed backend.

This Privacy Policy explains what information the Service processes, why, and how it is protected. It applies only to the Service — the technical integration — and not to the Hospital's medical records or clinical systems. Messages that patients and other people exchange with the Hospital over WhatsApp are also governed by Meta's and WhatsApp's own terms and privacy policies.

You can visit the official hospital website at https://truecarehospitals.com/.

2. Information the Service processes

The Service stores only the information it needs to run the WhatsApp integration. The categories below match the integration's actual database schema and server code.

2.1 Operator (staff) account data

To sign in the Hospital's staff ("operators") who run WhatsApp onboarding, the Service uses email and password authentication. It processes:

  • The operator's email address and an authentication credential managed by our authentication provider (passwords are handled by that provider and are not stored by the Service in readable form).
  • A generated operator user identifier (a UUID) assigned by the authentication provider.
  • The operator's role: owner, admin, or viewer.

2.2 WhatsApp Business account and number identifiers

When an operator completes Meta's Embedded Signup, the Service reads and stores identifiers that Meta returns for the Hospital's account:

  • Business account identifier and WhatsApp Business Account ("WABA") identifier.
  • Phone number identifier, display phone number, and the verified business name.
  • Platform type, the business-app coexistence flag, account status, and an onboarding timestamp.

2.3 Onboarding session data

To link a signup attempt to the correct operator, the Service issues a single-use, random nonce. It stores the nonce with the operator user identifier, an expiry time (10 minutes), and the time it was used.

2.4 Access credentials (tokens)

The Service exchanges Meta's authorization code, on the server, for a WhatsApp Business access token. That token is stored encrypted in a secure secret store. Only an opaque secret-store reference (a UUID) and non-sensitive lifecycle metadata (status such as none, active, expired, or revoked, plus expiry and last-rotated times) is stored in the Service's ordinary database tables. The raw token is never stored in ordinary tables and never reaches the browser page.

2.5 WhatsApp webhook event data

Meta sends webhook notifications to the Service for the connected WhatsApp Business Account (for example, an incoming-message notification or a message status change). For each event the Service may store:

  • The object type, event field/type, WABA identifier, and phone number identifier.
  • The message identifier, delivery/status value, and event timestamp.
  • A de-duplication key derived by hashing the event's identity fields, and whether the event's signature was valid.
  • Received and processed timestamps and a processing status.
  • A redacted copy of the event payload (see 2.6).

2.6 What the Service redacts before storing

Before any webhook payload is stored, the Service applies a redaction step in code:

  • Message text and bodies, captions, media, files, links/URLs, contact cards, names, email addresses, and physical addresses are removed entirely and are not stored.
  • Phone-number-like identifiers (for example from, to, wa_id, recipient_id, display_phone_number, phone_number, customer_phone) are replaced with a short SHA-256 hash, not stored in plaintext.
  • Other string values are truncated to a maximum length, and nesting depth is capped, to limit what is retained.

2.7 Information the Service does not intentionally collect

The Service is not designed to collect medical records or other special-category information, and its redaction step is intended to strip message content, contact details, and raw phone numbers before storage.

3. How the Service uses information

  • To sign in operators and authorise their actions by role (owner, admin, viewer).
  • To complete Meta Embedded Signup and store the resulting access token securely.
  • To confirm that the WhatsApp Business Account and phone number being connected match the assets the operator granted.
  • To receive, verify, de-duplicate, and record WhatsApp webhook events for the connected number.
  • To keep operational and security logs (for example, rejected webhook signatures).
  • To meet legal obligations and enforce our terms.

4. Meta and WhatsApp

The Service uses Meta's WhatsApp Business Platform and Graph API to: run Meta Embedded Signup (Facebook Login for Business); exchange the resulting authorization code for a business access token; read the connected WhatsApp Business Account and phone number identifiers for verification; and subscribe the integration to receive webhook notifications. Meta processes that data under its own terms and privacy policy. Meta is an independent controller for the data it processes on its platform.

5. Service providers

The Service relies on established managed backend and infrastructure providers that supply the managed PostgreSQL database, authentication, server-side application functions, and encrypted secret storage used by the Service. These providers process this data on the Hospital's behalf under their own terms and privacy policies.

7. How the Service protects information

The Service puts the following technical measures in place. This is a factual description and is not a guarantee of absolute security; no method of electronic storage or transmission is completely secure.

  • Webhook authenticity is verified using Meta's HMAC-SHA256 signature header (X-Hub-Signature-256) over the exact raw request body, with a timing-safe comparison.
  • Authorization codes and access tokens are exchanged and stored server-side; the raw token is held only in encrypted secret storage and referenced by an opaque UUID.
  • Database tables have row-level security forced on and are reached through server-side functions rather than direct client access.
  • Operator actions are authorised by role; only operators with the owner role can perform token operations.
  • Application secrets (Meta app credentials, verification token, and service credentials) are held as server-side secrets and are not placed in client pages or the public repository.

8. How long information is kept

We keep information only as long as needed to run the Service, meet legal obligations, and resolve disputes:

  • Onboarding session records expire after 10 minutes, and expired sessions are removed by the Service.
  • Account and asset identifiers are kept while the number remains connected through the Service.
  • Access tokens are held in encrypted storage until they are revoked (at which point they are deleted from our secret store) or until they expire.
  • Webhook event records are kept for operational and security purposes.

Not yet finalised: specific retention periods are not defined in the Service's code or configuration. See Details pending confirmation. We do not state a retention period we cannot support.

9. User data deletion instructions

You can ask us to delete the information the Service holds about you. This section explains how to request deletion, how we verify a request, and what we can and cannot delete.

How to submit a deletion request

  1. Email truecarehospitalsfrontdesk@gmail.com with the subject line "Data Deletion Request".
  2. Include the WhatsApp Business phone number involved (the Hospital number is +91 6360-166-366).
  3. If your request is about a staff/operator account, include the operator email address.
  4. Briefly describe what you want deleted (for example, "delete my operator account and any webhook records for my number").

How we verify a request

To protect the account, we confirm that the requester is authorised before we act — for example, by confirming control of the relevant WhatsApp Business number or operator account, or by another reasonable method. We may ask for additional confirmation before deleting anything.

What we can delete

We can delete the information the Service stores in the integration backend, including: operator account records, onboarding session records, WhatsApp Business account and number identifiers, stored access tokens (which we revoke and remove from our secret store), and webhook event records.

What we cannot delete

The Service does not control data that lives on Meta's or WhatsApp's own systems — for example, your WhatsApp message history, backups on your device, and your WhatsApp Business account data. To delete that data, use WhatsApp's in-app controls or Meta's data-deletion process. Because the Service redacts and does not store message content, there is generally no message content held by the Service to delete.

What happens next

After we verify a request, we delete or de-identify the relevant records and revoke any stored token, and we confirm the outcome to you. We may keep a minimal record of the request itself where the law requires it.
